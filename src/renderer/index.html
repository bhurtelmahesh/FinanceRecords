    How every number is worked out

    Amounts are yen. Green means money gained or on track, red means money lost, owed or falling short — the same rule everywhere in the app. On the dashboard charts, falling short means take-home under half of gross pay, or a stock total below its target.

    Salary and stock are kept apart. Salary & Savings and Overtime are the salary side. Daily Records and Stock Revenue are the stock side. Stock movements are never netted off your pay or expenditures.

    Payslips

    Overtime Pay
    The total of your Overtime entries for that month. Logged overtime wins: the figure typed here is used only for months with no overtime entries.
    Gross Total
    Basic + Allowance + Overtime Pay + Transportation.
    Total Deduction
    Health Insurance + Pension + Employment Insurance + Residence Tax + Income Tax.
    Net Received
    Gross Total − Total Deduction.
    Income Tax
    Your own figure is kept whenever you enter one. Leave it blank and the app estimates it.

    Gross Total, Total Deduction and Net Received are always recalculated on save, so they are shown read-only.

    Income tax estimate

    Used only when Income Tax is left blank. It annualises Basic + Allowance + Overtime (Transportation is excluded, as commuting allowance is not taxable), subtracts the employment-income deduction, then social insurance and the basic deduction, applies the tax brackets with the surtax, and divides by twelve.

    Treat this as a rough guide — it ignores bonuses and will not match a payslip exactly. Enter the figure from your payslip whenever you have it.

    Daily Records

    What a number means
    This is the day-by-day stock log: a gain is positive, a loss negative. It rolls up into the Stock section and is never subtracted from your salary.
    A status instead of a number
    Type a word instead of an amount — CLOSED for a holiday, for example — and the day is stored with a zero amount and that marker.
    Zero versus empty
    A cell showing 0 is a day recorded as zero. An empty cell has no record at all. Clearing a cell deletes its record.
    Saturday and Sunday
    Shown as a red chip until you enter something. Weekends are fully editable.
    Monthly Total
    The sum of that month’s amounts. A dash means the month has no records at all.

    Salary & Savings

    Gross Income
    The month’s Gross Total — the same Total shown on the Overtime page.
    Take-home
    What actually reached you: Gross Income − Total Deduction.
    Actual Savings
    Take-home − all Expenditure records assigned to that month. Negative savings are allowed.
    Savings Goal
    Whatever you set. It is never calculated.
    What counts as a month so far
    Simply whether the month has happened yet, by the calendar. A bonus counts from the month it is paid: the year’s first bonus from June, a later one from December.
    Cumulative Capital
    A running total of Actual Savings after expenditures: each month carries on from the one before it, and every new year starts again from zero.

    Rows generated from Salary Details cannot be deleted here — delete the matching salary detail instead. Months that have not happened yet are shaded and tagged projected: the figures are the ones you entered ahead of time, and the dashboard keeps them out of its headline totals.

    Expenditures

    Expense items
    Add any number of dated items with a category, amount and optional note. Monthly and annual totals update automatically.
    How expenses affect savings
    Each item is deducted once from take-home in its calendar month. Debt records stay separate and are never counted automatically, preventing double-counting.
    Bonuses
    The first bonus is assigned to June and later bonuses to December before that month’s expenditures are deducted.

    Dashboard

    Each figure is a button: click it to open the section it comes from.

    Salary · Income
    Gross pay for elapsed months, with take-home shown in the supporting line. Future salary figures are labelled as projections.
    Savings
    Elapsed take-home minus elapsed expenditures. Its supporting line shows both figures, and negative savings are red.
    Stock · Win Total
    The latest Actual Win Cumulative recorded that year. The tile is red below target and green on or above target; distance from target changes intensity, and the caption always states the gap.
    Outstanding Debt
    Every Debt Amount added together, exactly as entered. The caption counts the records and the distinct lenders behind that total.
    Salary chart
    Gross income in blue and take-home in green, month by month. Take-home turns red, marked ▼, wherever it falls below half of gross pay. Each bonus has its own shaded column, placed where it was paid. The axis runs from about ¥50k below the lowest month to ¥50k above the highest, rather than from zero, so month-to-month changes are easier to see.
    Stock chart
    Actual Win Cumulative against Target Cumulative in gold. Actual is green, marked ▲, on or above target and red, marked ▼, below it, with the gap between the two lines shaded to match. The latest figure is labelled at the end of the line.
    Months still to come
    Dashed on both charts, with hollow markers. On the Salary chart they are the projected months that the headline totals leave out.
    Hover and zoom
    Hover or tap a month for its figures. Scroll or pinch on a chart to zoom in on a few months, drag sideways to move along the year, and double-click or press Reset to see the whole year again. Zoomed in, the axis fits the months in view.

    Stock Revenue

    Monthly Win
    This month’s Actual Win Cumulative − the previous recorded month’s. The first month with a figure shows zero, as there is nothing to compare against.
    Vs Target
    Actual Win Cumulative − Target Cumulative.
    Result
    A tick above target, a cross below, a dash when the month has no actual figure or lands exactly on target.

    Overtime

    Amount
    (OT Hours + Extra OT) × Hourly Rate.
    Monthly OT Summary
    Adds that month’s entries and shows them against the matching salary detail, so a payslip can be checked before entering it.

    Backups and workbooks

    Backup (.json)
    Everything: every record, plus the archived salary sheets and bill files. This is the one to keep. Importing a backup replaces all current records.
    Workbook (.xlsx)
    A spreadsheet of the records only, one sheet per menu, with a Summary sheet of the dashboard figures. Archived files are listed but their contents are not included, so a workbook is not a substitute for a backup.

    Starting from a spreadsheet? Export a workbook first, even with no records in it. That gives you the exact sheet names and column headings the importer expects — fill those sheets in, then import the file back. Building a spreadsheet from scratch and hoping it matches will not work.

    Importing a workbook replaces the current records too, so export a backup before you try it. Rows come back in as new entries, so a workbook is best for setting records up or reworking them in bulk rather than for merging into what is already there.

    Account & sync

    Sign in with the same Google account as the web app to sync financial records through Firebase. The desktop keeps a separate per-account cache, so changes remain available when the network is down.

    Local mode keeps its own finance-data.json and is restored when you sign out. Salary sheets and unpaid-bill files never upload; they remain only on this Mac and are included in native JSON backups.

    Finance Tracker on the web

    The same app runs in a browser at finance-records.web.app, so the records you keep here are with you on a phone, a tablet, or someone else’s computer.

    Getting your records there
    Sign in with the same Google account in both places and they carry across. A record edited in two places keeps whichever was saved last.
    Without signing in
    Export a JSON backup here and import it there. That moves everything, including archived salary sheets and bill files, which sync never uploads.
    On a phone
    The web version installs like an app: Share › Add to Home Screen on iPhone and iPad, or Chrome’s menu › Install app on Android. It then opens from your home screen with its own icon.

    The link opens in your usual browser — this app never browses the web itself.

    Your data

    Everything is stored in finance-data.json on this Mac. Signed out, nothing leaves it; signed in, financial records sync to your own account while salary sheets and bill files stay here. Export a backup regularly — and before clearing anything.
